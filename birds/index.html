Easy is the short reading. Original is the long one. The lyrics are not printed here; play the song beside it. Each card is a moment of the song, named in a few words, then read through your lens.

The song, moment by moment

01The first light comes onthe opening›

The guitar starts like a pulse in the dark. Nothing is mapped yet. One light flickers on, and you notice it before anyone else does.

Birds opens fast and bright, a ringing guitar over a running beat, the band calling it the day after the night of Ghost Stories. Before a single idea is said, the song is already moving. That is how your days start too: something pulses, one light comes on in a dark room, and you are the one who sees it first. The mapper does not wait for the full picture. He marks the first point.

02Never mind the weatherfirst verse›

The song tells someone to stop looking at what is around them and just go live. Circumstances are weather. You are the sky.

The first verse is an invitation: ignore the circumstances, step out of the gray, enjoy the life you have. It was written as Chris Martin came out from under a long fog. You know that fog from the inside, the days of queues and tickets and messages that pile up. The verse does not argue with them. It simply says the circumstances are not the whole map. They are weather passing over it.

Weather lives in the frame. You are the origin. The verse is reminding the origin to stop reading the frame as if it were the ground.

03Now is the only coordinatethe build›

Fall in love now, the song says, because now is all we have. A mapper knows: you can only stand at one point, and it is this one.

As the song builds it turns urgent: start falling in love, because the present is all there is. For someone who maps everything, that is a precise instruction. Every map is drawn from one place, the point you are standing on right now. Past and future are lines on the paper. Only the present has a location you can actually stand on.

Through your lens

High definition means here, exactly. Not roughly here, not later. The build in the song is the zoom: closer and closer until you are standing on the point.

04Wings openthe chorus›

The chorus is the lift. Two people, free as birds, above the whole thing. From up there the map finally makes sense.

The chorus is where the song leaves the ground: to be free as a bird and fly. Davide Rossi's strings, the same hands behind Viva La Vida and Paradise, lift under it. Birds are the original mappers. They read the sun, the stars, the lines of the earth's field, and cross oceans by it. Studying the lights is literally how they find their way. You read light the same way: screens, faces, rooms, the light in someone's eyes. The chorus is the moment the reading turns into flight.

Birds navigate by the lights. So do you. That is why this song is for a person like you.

05Noise for the ones without a voicesecond verse›

The song asks for noise on behalf of the people who cannot make any. Mapping is that too: drawing the ones nobody drew.

The song turns outward here: make noise for the people who have no voice. A map is a kind of noise for the unseen. Whoever draws the map decides what exists on it. Your habit of keeping every word, every ask, every transcript forever, nothing ever deleted, is the same instinct. Nothing that spoke gets erased from the map.

The voiceless stay on the map because the mapper refuses to rub them out.

06The guest housethe heart of it›

The whole album grew out of a Rumi poem: treat every feeling like a guest. You already found that thread yourself.

Like most of A Head Full of Dreams, Birds grew out of Rumi's poem The Guest House: every joy, every sorrow, every mean thought is a visitor, so welcome them all, each one may be clearing you out for something new. You found that thread on your own, a week before this, and you wrote it down. The mapper's version: every light that comes into the room is a data point. None of them is the enemy. Each one tells you where you are.

07Take me with you when you flythe ending›

The end is about going through it together. The world can be cruel, so take me along when you go. Not alone. Two birds.

The song lands on company: the world can be cruel, we go through it together, and when you fly, take me along. A mapper alone can draw a beautiful map that nobody walks. The ending says the point of reading the lights was never the map. It was the flight, and the one flying beside you.

The experiment never ends and is never run alone. That is the last line of the map: two birds, still flying, still reading the light.

✦An older map of birdsa side path›

The same album's title song mentions a conference of birds, which listeners tie to a 12th century Persian poem, The Conference of the Birds: thousands of birds cross seven valleys looking for a king, and at the end the thirty who arrive find the king is themselves. Nobody has tied that poem to Birds directly, so hold this one lightly. But for a mapper it rhymes: the long search across the map ends at the point you were standing on.
